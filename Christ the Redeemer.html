Christ the Redeemer

Overview

Christ the Redeemer is a famous Art Deco statue that stands atop Corcovado Mountain in Rio de Janeiro. Completed in 1931, it became a global symbol of Christianity and Brazilian culture, and it is one of the most recognizable landmarks in South America. With its open arms and towering position above the city, it represents both spiritual welcome and national pride.

Historical Background

The idea for a statue on Corcovado Mountain was proposed in the mid-1850s, although the project was not completed until the 1920s. The Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rio de Janeiro launched a campaign to fund the statue, and the foundation stone was laid in 1922. The design was shaped by Brazilian creativity and engineering, combining faith, modern construction, and a strong sense of public identity.
